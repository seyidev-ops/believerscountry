This Compendium is not an executive summary. It is the governing corpus for the formation of CMI leadership. A leader who completes this material should be able to state the CMI position, show the scriptural basis for it, distinguish it from inherited religious language, answer the obvious objections, teach it without dependence on the founder, and translate doctrine into the life of a local embassy.
	THE STANDARD OF COMPLETION
A conclusion without its scriptural route is incomplete. A doctrine without its operational consequence is unfinished. A vocabulary change without the theological reason behind it is cosmetic. CMI therefore teaches from evidence to conclusion, and from conclusion to practice.


How to Read This Corpus

Every leader must distinguish doctrine, house terminology and operational convention. Doctrine requires scriptural warrant; terminology serves doctrine; operational conventions may change without changing doctrine.

PART I — ORIGIN, IDENTITY & THE COUNTRY CONSTRUCT
1. HISTORICAL CONTEXT & FOUNDATIONAL ORIGINS
Believers Country emerged from a sustained period of scriptural re-examination rather than from a desire to add another denomination to the existing church landscape. The governing question was not, “How do we improve church as we have received it?” but, “What does the New Testament actually say has happened to the person who is in Christ, and what kind of corporate life follows if we take that reality seriously?”
The result is Country Ministry International (CMI): an umbrella ministry structure under which the realities of citizenship, scriptural formation, commonwealth and deployment are organized. Believers Country (BC) is the primary active national domain within that structure.
1.1 Restoration Rather Than Rebranding
CMI does not begin by changing labels. It begins by changing the governing frame. The believer is not treated principally as a religious attendee waiting to leave earth. The believer is treated as one who has been transferred, born from above, made a citizen, indwelt by the Spirit and commissioned to represent the present Lordship of Christ in the earth.
Colossians 1:13 — the believer has been rescued from the authority of darkness and transferred into the domain of the Son.
Philippians 3:20 — the believer’s politeuma, commonwealth/citizenship, is in heaven, from which the Saviour is awaited.
Ephesians 2:19 — believers are no longer strangers and aliens but fellow citizens with the saints and members of God’s household.
Hebrews 11:13–16 — the faithful confess themselves strangers on earth because they seek a better patris, a homeland/country, prepared by God.
1 Peter 2:9–12 — a royal priesthood and holy nation is formed in order to proclaim the excellencies of the One who called them out of darkness.
These texts do not describe a loose religious association. They describe transfer, citizenship, household, nation, homeland and representation. CMI’s country vocabulary is an attempt to allow those political and civic metaphors to retain their force rather than flattening them into church attendance.
1.2 The Inaugural Convocation — 1 Tishrei 5787
The Hebrew year 5787 begins at sundown on Friday, 11 September 2026; its first civil day is Saturday, 12 September 2026. The inaugural physical convocation of CMI is therefore placed at the threshold of 1 Tishrei 5787, Yom Teruah / Rosh Hashanah, a day marked in Scripture by sacred rest, trumpet memorial and holy convocation (Leviticus 23:23–25; Numbers 29:1).
The date functions as an administrative sign: a people are called to assembly at the head of a year, and the year itself opens on Shabbat. CMI does not make the calendar the Gospel. The calendar serves the Gospel by disciplining attention, memory, assembly and ordered time.
2. COUNTRY, COMMONWEALTH AND THE QUESTION OF “KINGDOM”
CMI’s use of Country is not a claim that the Greek word basileia literally means country. Basileia ordinarily denotes reign, kingship, royal rule or kingdom. The CMI move is conceptual and administrative: where inherited English usage has made “kingdom” sound abstract, ecclesiastical or future-only, CMI restores the concrete civic consequences of divine reign through the New Testament’s own citizenship, commonwealth, nation and homeland vocabulary.

CMI therefore uses Country as a house term for the lived civic reality produced by the reign of God, and Commonwealth where the emphasis is shared citizenship, welfare and belonging. The term does not erase kingship; it makes kingship inhabitable. There is a King, therefore there is a rule. There is a rule, therefore there is a people ordered under it. There is a people, therefore there is citizenship, culture, economy, responsibility and representation.
	LEADERSHIP GUARDRAIL
Do not teach that every occurrence of “kingdom” in an English Bible should be lexically translated “country.” Teach instead that CMI uses Country to recover the concrete citizenship and homeland implications that Scripture itself supplies through basileia, politeuma, sympolites, ethnos and patris.


2.3 BELIEVERS COUNTRY: OUR RECEIVED IDENTITY AND CORPORATE EXPRESSION

Public identity declaration

A people belonging to the Father. A life received in the Son. A country expressed through His Spirit.

The Question

What constitutes Believers Country? Is it a gathering, a geographic institution, a spiritual family, a culture, a programme, or something that the Father has brought into being through the Son and makes visible by His Spirit?

Scriptural Investigation — Ephesians 2:18–22

Read Paul's argument as a whole, including the reconciliation of those previously separated (Ephesians 2:11–17). The movement in verse 18 is through Christ, by one Spirit, to the Father. Paul's next step is communal: those who have gained access are no longer outsiders; they are fellow citizens and members of God's household. They are being built together in Christ as a dwelling place of God in the Spirit. The same passage therefore connects access to the Father, the mediation of the Son, the operation of the Spirit, and a corporate belonging. It is stronger for CMI's overall identity than John 1:4 standing alone.

What the Evidence Establishes

Believers Country can coherently describe a people who belong to God and share a common life in Christ, visibly formed together by the Spirit. The word country is CMI's deliberate identity language for communal belonging, culture, mutual life and shared service; it is not a claim that the Greek word basileia literally means “country”, not a new political territory, and not a replacement for the universal body of Christ. The Church belongs to Christ; CMI is one local ministry expression within it.

2.3.1 A PEOPLE BELONGING TO THE FATHER

Principal texts: 1 John 3:1–2; Romans 8:14–17; Galatians 4:4–7; Ephesians 2:18–19; Ephesians 1:4–6.

The Father is not an abstract destination behind the Son; He is the One to whom the Son brings us. Scripture presents the believer's relation to the Father as received sonship and adoption, not an earned status bought by ministry performance. Galatians 4 places redemption through the Son before the believer's cry of “Abba, Father”; Romans 8 describes the Spirit testifying to the children of God. Thus “belonging” is covenantal and familial before it is institutional or administrative. We gather because we are received; we do not earn our reception by attending a gathering.

Guardrail: Do not suggest that the Father belongs to CMI exclusively, or that membership in Believers Country is equivalent to salvation or divine adoption. Belonging to the Father is grounded in Christ, not organizational registration.

2.3.2 A LIFE RECEIVED IN THE SON

Principal texts: John 14:19–23; John 17:20–23; 1 John 5:11–12; John 1:12–13; Colossians 3:3–4; Galatians 2:20.

The life of the believer is not a competing life added alongside Christ. It is the life received in Him. John 14 joins Christ's living, the believer's living and mutual indwelling; 1 John 5 locates eternal life in the Son. The statement “a life received in the Son” therefore speaks first of participation in the life of Christ, secured by His person and finished work, rather than a religious quality achieved through human discipline. Conduct, community and service arise as expressions of that life, not its purchase price.

Guardrail: Do not turn “life received” into immunity from weakness, suffering or responsible stewardship. Do not make manifestations, feelings or visible success the evidence of possessing eternal life. Christ is the ground, and Scripture supplies the test.

2.3.3 A COUNTRY EXPRESSED THROUGH HIS SPIRIT

Principal texts: Ephesians 2:19–22; Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:14–16; 1 Corinthians 12:4–13; Galatians 5:22–25; John 16:13–14; Ephesians 4:1–6.

The Spirit does not establish a second identity separate from Christ. He bears witness to sonship, joins believers in one body, distributes gifts for the common good, forms Christlike fruit and directs the gathered people toward the truth of the Son. Expression means that the shared life becomes recognizable: how people speak, welcome, serve, teach, pray, handle material responsibility and carry the Gospel to others. The country is not sustained by atmosphere, symbols, events or personalities; it is expressed through Spirit-formed life consistent with Christ.

Guardrail: The Spirit is a Person, never the ministry's brand accessory or a tool for compulsion. The existing Holy Spirit manual explicitly rejects manipulation under His name and teaches that His ministry glorifies the Son. Never imply that CMI leaders control Him or that a person's standing can be measured by spectacular experiences. Gifts exist for the common good and must be exercised with love and accountability.

2.3.4 CMI POSITION AND GUARDRAILS

CMI holds: The Father welcomes believers as His children through the Son; eternal life is received in the Son; the Spirit bears witness, forms and expresses that life in the gathered body; the resulting fellowship is visible in love, truth, holiness, teaching, prayer, practical care and mission. All ministry expressions are accountable to the Son as foundation and interpretive centre.

CMI rejects: Institutional membership as the ground of belonging to the Father; human performance as the origin of eternal life; Spirit-led language divorced from the character and teaching of Jesus; a national-sovereignty reading of “Country”; magical or automatic claims built upon slogans rather than the Scripture that supports them.

2.3.5 CMI MINISTRY EXPRESSIONS

- Believers Country: the shared community that welcomes believers into fellowship and responsibility.
- School of Scriptures: deep scriptural formation in the light of Christ, with context and confirmation before conclusion.
- Commonwealth: responsible material care, stewardship and practical welfare, subject to the actual programmes CMI authorizes.
- Country Vanguard: outreach, mission, deployment and service beyond the gathering, under the lordship of the Son.

These are distinct operations of one community, not four sources of spiritual life. Avoid inventing programmes, guaranteed outcomes or theological rank between the streams.

2.3.6 LEADERSHIP EXAMINATION QUESTIONS

1. Can a leader demonstrate from Ephesians 2:18–22 that access to the Father, mediation through Christ, operation by the Spirit and corporate belonging belong in one argument?
2. Can the leader distinguish divine sonship from membership in a local ministry?
3. Can the leader show from John 14 and 1 John 5 why eternal life is received in the Son rather than attained by performance?
4. Can the leader explain how the Spirit's work makes the common life visible without making gifts or experiences the measure of salvation?
5. Can the leader defend the identity word “Country” without claiming that basileia literally translates as “country” or that CMI has political sovereignty?

PART II — THE CENTRAL MESSAGE: YESHUA IS SALVATION
3. SALVATION IS NOT FIRST A PROCESS; SALVATION IS A PERSON
The Question
What exactly is announced when CMI says, “Yeshua is Salvation”? Is this merely rhetoric, or does Scripture identify salvation so completely with the Son that to receive Him is to receive life?
Scriptural Investigation
Luke 2:25–32 — Simeon takes the child Yeshua in his arms and says, ‘my eyes have seen Your salvation.’ The salvation he sees is not a doctrine placed beside the child; it is present in the child.
Luke 19:9–10 — when Yeshua enters Zacchaeus’ house, He announces that salvation has come to that house. Salvation arrives because the Son has arrived.
John 1:4 — ‘in Him was life.’ Life is located in the Person before it is described as a benefit.
John 10:9 — Yeshua does not merely show a door; He says, ‘I am the door.’ John 14:6 likewise: ‘I am the way, the truth, and the life.’
Acts 4:10–12 — salvation is located in no other, because no other name has been given by which we must be saved.
1 John 5:11–12 — eternal life is ‘in His Son’; having the Son and having life are treated as the same decisive possession.
Hebrews 5:9 — the Son is named the source/cause of eternal salvation to those who obey Him.
What the Evidence Establishes
The New Testament consistently refuses to separate the saving benefits from the saving Person. Forgiveness, life, reconciliation, righteousness and deliverance are not autonomous commodities distributed apart from Christ. They are received in union with Him.
CMI Position
CMI announces Yeshua Himself. We do not preach a salvation mechanism with Jesus added as the required password. The Gospel is the proclamation of the crucified, risen and enthroned Son. To receive Him is to receive the life that is in Him.
Leadership Consequence
A CMI leader must be able to move a hearer from “What must I do to obtain a religious status?” to “Who is the Son, what has He accomplished, and am I in Him?” The centre of evangelism is therefore Christ, not an administrative formula.
4. CHRIST AS THE CENTRE OF THE WHOLE SCRIPTURAL WITNESS
CMI’s Christophanic reading begins with Yeshua’s own handling of Scripture. After the resurrection He does not treat Moses and the Prophets as an independent message that happens to predict Him occasionally. He interprets “in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself” (Luke 24:27), and then names the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms as witnesses that must be fulfilled concerning Him (Luke 24:44–47).
John 5:39–40 — the Scriptures testify about Him, yet the hearers refuse to come to Him that they may have life. Scripture can be studied while its centre is missed.
John 1:45 — Philip announces the One “of whom Moses in the Law, and also the Prophets, wrote.”
Acts 3:18–24 — Peter reads the prophets as announcing the sufferings and times of Messiah.
Acts 8:30–35 — Philip begins with Isaiah and preaches Yeshua.
Acts 17:2–3 — Paul reasons from the Scriptures that Messiah had to suffer and rise, and identifies Yeshua as that Messiah.
2 Corinthians 1:20 — the promises of God find their Yes in Him.
Colossians 2:16–17 — calendar shadows are distinguished from the substance/body that belongs to Christ.
Hebrews 10:1 — the Law contains a shadow of coming good things; the argument moves to the once-for-all offering of Christ.
Revelation 19:10 — the testimony of Yeshua is the spirit of prophecy.
4.1 The Hermeneutical Order
CMI therefore reads Scripture in an order: text, Christ, accomplishment, New Creation, operation. The Old Testament is not discarded. It is fulfilled, unveiled and administratively interpreted through the Son. The Gospels reveal the Son in incarnation and mission; Acts reveals the enthroned Son acting by the Spirit through His witnesses; the Epistles explain the accomplished work and the identity of those in Him; Revelation unveils the Lamb in present and final governance.
4.2 Earthly Controversy and Citizen Doctrine
Not every sentence spoken during Yeshua’s earthly ministry is addressed to the same audience or covenantal situation. He speaks to disciples, crowds, Pharisees, rulers, individuals under Torah, and opponents testing Him. CMI therefore refuses to turn every controversy statement into an undifferentiated rule for New Creation citizens. Audience, covenantal location and purpose matter. The Epistles are especially important because they explain the consequences of the cross, resurrection, ascension and gift of the Spirit after those events have occurred.
	THE RULE
The Epistles do not replace Yeshua; they are the apostolic explanation of what Yeshua accomplished. Revelation does not introduce another Christ; it unveils the crucified Lamb as the enthroned Lord. The canon remains one witness, centred in one Person.


5. ETERNAL SALVATION AND CONTINUOUS BELIEVING
CMI rejects the slogan-war between “once saved, always saved” and a salvation maintained by anxious self-preservation. Both can misplace the centre. The decisive subject of eternal salvation is Christ: His life is eternal, His priesthood is permanent, His sacrifice is sufficient, and the believer’s life is located in Him.
5.1 The Security Texts
John 5:24 — the one hearing and believing has eternal life and has passed from death into life.
John 10:27–30 — the sheep hear, follow and are held; no one can snatch them from the Son’s or Father’s hand.
Romans 8:31–39 — no created thing can separate those in Christ from the love of God in Christ.
Hebrews 7:24–25 — because His priesthood is permanent, He is able to save completely those who draw near through Him.
Hebrews 10:10–14 — by one offering He has perfected for all time those being sanctified.
1 Peter 1:3–5 — an imperishable inheritance is kept, and believers are guarded through faith.
5.2 The Abiding and Continuing Texts
John 15:4–6 — the branch is commanded to abide in the vine; fruit is impossible apart from union.
Colossians 1:21–23 — reconciliation is announced, followed by the call to continue in the faith, stable and steadfast.
Hebrews 3:6, 14 — participation is described with perseverance language: holding confidence firm to the end.
1 Corinthians 15:1–2 — the Gospel is received, stood in and held fast.
1 John 2:24–25 — what was heard from the beginning is to abide in the believer; the promise is eternal life.
5.3 CMI Synthesis
Eternal life is not an independent substance deposited in a person so that Christ becomes unnecessary afterward. Eternal life remains in the Son. The believer’s assurance therefore rests in the sufficiency and keeping power of Christ, while the New Testament’s language of hearing, following, abiding, holding and continuing is allowed to remain real. CMI calls this continuous believing: not repeated re-salvation, but the ongoing posture of the believing one toward the eternal Son.
The pastoral result is neither terror nor presumption. The believer does not wake each morning trying to become saved again. Neither does the believer use a past religious moment to make present unbelief irrelevant. We remain where life is: in the Son.
PART III — THE BELIEVER, CITIZENSHIP & HOUSE LANGUAGE
6. WHY CMI PREFERS “BELIEVER”
The New Testament does use the word Christianos, and CMI must not deny that fact. It appears in Acts 11:26, Acts 26:28 and 1 Peter 4:16. The question is not whether “Christian” is a biblical word. The question is which word best carries the identity CMI intends to form.
6.1 The Language of Believing
The Johannine writings repeatedly organize response to Yeshua around the verb pisteuō — to believe, trust, entrust oneself. John 3:16 does not describe a social label but the believing one. John 6:47 locates eternal life in the one believing. John 20:31 states the purpose of the Gospel: that readers may believe Yeshua is Messiah, the Son of God, and by believing have life in His name.
The Epistles also use pistos for faithful/believing persons and adelphoi, saints, elect, disciples and members of Christ’s body. CMI chooses Believer as its primary citizen designation because it keeps the defining act and posture visible: this is the believing one, the one whose life is ordered by trust in the Son.
6.2 What We Do With “Christian”
Acts 11:26 records that the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch. Acts 26:28 places the term on Agrippa’s lips. 1 Peter 4:16 can receive the designation positively: if anyone suffers as a Christian, let that person not be ashamed but glorify God in that name. Therefore CMI does not teach that the word itself is pagan contamination or unusable Scripture. We simply do not make it our controlling house identity.
	CMI HOUSE RULE
Use Believer as the primary identity term. When teaching why, argue from the New Testament’s emphasis on believing and union with Christ, not from a claim that the New Testament forbids the word Christian.


7. CITIZENSHIP IS AN IDENTITY BEFORE IT IS AN ORGANIZATION
A Believers Country citizen is first a person in Christ. Membership in a CMI structure does not create citizenship in God’s realm. The structure recognizes, trains and organizes what the Gospel has already created. This distinction protects CMI from replacing Christ with institution.
New birth precedes civic formation (John 3:3–8).
Transfer precedes administration (Colossians 1:13).
Fellow citizenship is given in Christ, not sold by an institution (Ephesians 2:13–22).
The Spirit constitutes one body before local organization assigns functions (1 Corinthians 12:12–13).
Leadership equips saints; it does not manufacture their access to God (Ephesians 4:11–16).
PART IV — LIVING ETERNAL LIFE IN ABUNDANCE
8. ZOE — THE LIFE RECEIVED
Zōē in John is not merely biological existence. John places it in the Son: “In Him was life” (John 1:4); the Father has life in Himself and grants the Son to have life in Himself (John 5:26); the Son gives eternal life to His sheep (John 10:28); and 1 John 5:11–12 locates eternal life in the Son.
CMI therefore speaks of Zoe as the divine life shared through union with Christ. We should be precise: the believer does not become self-existent deity. The believer participates in life that has its source in God and is possessed in the Son. Dependence is not a defect in Zoe; dependence on the Son is its form for the creature.
9. PERISSON — THE LIFE OVERFLOWING
John 10:10 places the thief’s work of stealing, killing and destroying against the Shepherd’s purpose: “I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly.” Perisson carries the sense of beyond the ordinary measure, more than enough, exceeding. CMI uses it to name the outward fullness of the life already received in Christ.
Abundance is therefore not reduced to money. It includes spiritual vitality, wisdom, bodily stewardship, productive work, generosity, relational health, courage, service and the capacity to become a source rather than a drain. Material provision belongs inside that field, but it is not allowed to define the field.

PART V — THE HOLY SPIRIT, MINISTERING TO THE LORD & SUMMONS
10. THE HOLY SPIRIT IS GOD AND PERSON
CMI rejects language that reduces the Holy Spirit to power, atmosphere, electricity, “anointing” as a substance, or a tool operated by gifted people. Scripture gives Him personal agency: He speaks (Acts 13:2), wills (1 Corinthians 12:11), teaches and reminds (John 14:26), can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30), searches (1 Corinthians 2:10), intercedes (Romans 8:26–27) and appoints overseers (Acts 20:28). Acts 5:3–4 places lying to the Holy Spirit in direct relation to lying to God.
10.1 His Mission is Christ-Centred
John 15:26 — He testifies about Yeshua.
John 16:13–15 — He does not glorify Himself; He glorifies the Son and discloses what belongs to Him.
Acts 2:32–36 — the exalted Son receives the promise of the Spirit and pours out what is seen and heard. Pentecost therefore witnesses to enthronement.
1 Corinthians 12:3 — genuine spiritual speech confesses Jesus as Lord.
This gives CMI a test for spirituality: whatever claims to be “Spirit” but competes with the finished work, obscures the Son, magnifies the vessel, or produces disorder contrary to Scripture has failed the Spirit’s own stated mission.

11. MINISTERING TO THE LORD
Acts 13:1–3 provides the governing pattern. Prophets and teachers in Antioch are “ministering to the Lord and fasting” when the Holy Spirit speaks: “Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.” The order matters. They are not first trying to extract an answer. Their Godward ministry becomes the environment in which a corporate instruction is heard.
CMI uses Ministering to the Lord to correct a prayer culture dominated by begging, crisis reaction and self-reference. This does not abolish petition; Scripture commands asking. It restores a neglected orientation in which worship, tongues, thanksgiving, fasting, Scripture and attentive silence are offered Godward before the citizen turns every gathering into a request desk.
11.1 Tongues in This Framework
1 Corinthians 14:2 says the one speaking in a tongue speaks not to people but to God; verse 4 says that person edifies himself. Paul can say, “I will pray with the spirit and I will pray with the mind also” (14:15), while also insisting that public congregational speech be intelligible and ordered (14:26–33, 39–40). CMI therefore makes room for tongues as Godward prayer while refusing to use private edification as permission for public disorder.
12. THE PROTOCOL OF SUMMONS
Summons is CMI house language for a specific divine instruction, burden, direction, warning, deployment or word received under the government of Scripture and the Holy Spirit. The term is deliberately stronger than “impression” because it carries response. A summons is not information to admire; it calls the citizen to obedience.
12.1 Scriptural Patterns Behind the Term
Acts 13:2–4 — the Spirit names persons and work; the community fasts, prays, lays hands and sends.
Acts 8:26–29 — an angel gives Philip a direction and the Spirit tells him to join the chariot; revelation becomes movement.
Acts 10:19–20 — Peter is told that men are seeking him and instructed to go without misgivings.
Acts 16:6–10 — restraint and a Macedonian vision redirect an apostolic team; guidance includes closed routes and opened routes.
Acts 20:22–24 — Paul describes himself as bound in spirit toward Jerusalem while aware of suffering; summons is not synonymous with comfort.
1 Timothy 1:18 — prophetic words can frame faithful warfare, but they operate under apostolic instruction rather than replacing it.
1 Thessalonians 5:19–22 — prophecy is not despised, but everything is tested and what is good is held fast.
1 Corinthians 14:29 — prophetic speech is weighed by others; revelation does not exempt itself from discernment.

PART VI — THE FOUR STREAMS OF NEW CREATION REALITY
13. ONE RIVER, FOUR STREAMS
Genesis 2:10 describes one river going out from Eden to water the garden and dividing into four heads. Revelation 22:1–2 closes the canon with the river of the water of life flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb, nourishing the tree whose leaves are for the healing of the nations. CMI uses the four-river architecture as an institutional picture: one life, one source, multiple functions.
The streams are not four competing ministries. They are four expressions of one country reality. If separated from the source, they become a church, a school, a charity and a missions agency. Held together, they become the civic ecology of Believers Country.

13.1 Perat — Believers Country
Believers Country is where doctrine becomes peoplehood. Its task is not merely to host weekly meetings but to form a recognizable citizenship culture: worship, teaching, communion, common responsibility, households, welfare, work and public witness.
Structure: Regional Strata → Local Embassies → Domestic Strata. The local embassy is not a franchise outlet. It is a governed local expression of the same message, life and civic order.
13.2 Gihon — School of Scriptures
The School exists because a movement that cannot reproduce its reasoning will eventually reproduce only its slogans. Its mandate is to teach leaders how conclusions are reached, not merely what conclusions to repeat. Luke 24, Acts 17:11 and 2 Timothy 2:15 establish the culture: Scripture opened around Christ, claims examined, workers trained to handle the word accurately.
Its curriculum therefore includes Christology, New Creation identity, the Holy Spirit, biblical languages and word studies, sacred time, CMI terminology, leadership formation and the responsible use of Hebrew-calendar/gematria frameworks.
13.3 Pishon — Commonwealth
The current public designation is Commonwealth. Earlier editions called this stream Country Commonwealth; its scriptural and operational purpose is unchanged.
Acts 2:44–47 and Acts 4:32–35 describe a community in which possession does not become indifference to need. The texts do not erase stewardship or private agency; they destroy the right to watch a brother or sister lack basic provision while resources sit unused. 2 Corinthians 8–9 adds voluntary generosity, proportionality, equality of burden and cheerful giving.
The Commonwealth therefore exists to make belonging economically visible: welfare, emergency support, productive empowerment, enterprise networks, skill exchange and asset stewardship. Its aim is not dependency. Its aim is that no citizen is abandoned and that receiving help becomes a route back to productive contribution wherever possible.
13.4 Hiddekel — Country Vanguard
The Vanguard carries the sending logic of Acts. The church at Antioch releases Barnabas and Saul; apostolic teams enter cities, preach Christ, form communities, appoint leadership and move. The Vanguard is therefore CMI’s deployment arm: pioneer envoys, embassy launches, mission teams, strategic logistics and rapid response.
Speed must remain governed. Hiddekel is a swift river, not an uncontrolled flood. Deployment follows doctrine, character, confirmation and accountable sending.
13.5 Interdependence
Believers Country without the School becomes culture without depth. The School without the Country becomes knowledge without peoplehood. Commonwealth without formation becomes welfare administration. Vanguard without all three becomes expansion without substance. The four streams correct one another by remaining one river.
PART VII — SACRED TIME, SHABBAT & THE 5787 BLUEPRINT
14. WHY TIME MATTERS
Scripture does not treat time as empty space. Genesis orders days; Leviticus names appointed times; the Gospels place the acts of Yeshua inside Passover and festival time; Acts marks Pentecost; Paul can distinguish shadow from substance while still recognizing the pedagogical force of days and seasons. CMI therefore uses sacred time as formation, not as a competing means of justification.
14.1 Evening and Morning
Genesis 1 repeatedly uses the sequence “evening and morning.” Leviticus 23:32 explicitly orders the Day of Atonement from evening to evening. CMI therefore begins its sacred-day observance at sundown. The practical significance is theological: rest is received before work is performed. The citizen enters the day from cessation, not from exhaustion.
14.2 Shabbat in CMI
Shabbat is not used as a ladder into righteousness. Hebrews 4 locates ultimate rest in God’s completed work and calls the people of God to enter that rest. Mark 2:27–28 places the Sabbath under the Son of Man and for human good. Colossians 2:16–17 warns against judgment over festival, new moon or Sabbath as though the shadow were the substance. CMI therefore receives Shabbat as a discipline of rest, assembly, Christ-centred teaching, communion, attention and weekly completion — never as a basis for justification.

15.1 Isaiah 49:2 as a Governing Confirmation
Isaiah 49:2 provides a striking textual sequence for the year: “He has made My mouth like a sharp sword… He has hidden Me in the shadow of His hand… He has made Me a polished arrow; in His quiver He has hidden Me.” CMI reads the passage through the four-letter architecture: Tav in ownership and concealment; Shin in polishing by friction; Pe in the made mouth; Zayin in the sharp edge.
The central leadership lesson is restraint. A year of the mouth is not a year of increased volume. The mouth is manufactured equipment. The sword arrives last because it can wound. PART IX — LEADERSHIP DEFENCE, OBJECTIONS & TRAINING STANDARD
18. QUESTIONS EVERY CMI LEADER MUST BE ABLE TO ANSWER
Are you saying the word “kingdom” is wrong?
No. Basileia is biblical and names God’s reign/kingship. CMI uses Country as a house term to recover the civic reality Scripture also names through citizenship, commonwealth, holy nation and homeland.
Are you saying Christians are not biblical?
No. Christianos occurs three times in the New Testament and 1 Peter can receive the name positively. CMI prefers Believer because believing is the repeated New Testament response to the Son and better serves our formation language.
Are you replacing the Gospel with Hebrew culture?
No. Christ is the centre and the New Creation is the controlling frame. Hebrew calendar and letter studies are formative lenses. They cannot create doctrine or compete with the finished work.
Does continuous believing mean salvation is lost every time faith feels weak?
No. Assurance rests in the eternal Son, His finished work and keeping power. Continuous believing means the believer’s life remains relationally located in the Son rather than treating a past moment as independence from Him.
Does “Yeshua is Salvation” deny justification, forgiveness or atonement?
No. It locates all of them where the New Testament locates them: in Christ and His accomplished work. The benefits are not denied; they are refused independence from the Benefactor.
Are Summons equal to Scripture?
No. Summons are present instructions claimed under the Spirit’s guidance. They must be tested by Scripture, Christ, fruit and community. Scripture governs the summons, not the reverse.
Does Priest restore Old Covenant clergy?
No. Christ is the final High Priest and all believers are a royal priesthood. CMI uses Priest functionally for altar and doctrinal oversight, not as a new mediator between citizens and God.
Is Shabbat required for justification?
No. CMI observes sacred time as formation and rest in Christ. Colossians 2 forbids judgment as though calendar observance were the substance; Christ is the substance.
Is abundant life a prosperity doctrine?
Not as CMI defines it. Perisson is the overflow of life in Christ across the whole person and community. Material provision matters, but money is not the measure of divine life.
Why four streams?
Because CMI needs one integrated ecology for peoplehood, teaching, commonwealth and deployment. Genesis 2 supplies the four-river picture; Revelation 22 returns the river to the throne and the healing of nations. The architecture is institutional, not a claim that Genesis named modern subsidiaries.

Before a leader completes this corpus, the leader should be able to teach the central message without notes, locate the principal confirmation texts, answer the ten objections above, conduct a Shabbat assembly, distinguish a summons from an impulse, explain the four streams, and train another citizen to do the same. The goal is not argumentative aggression. The goal is doctrinal readiness: the ability to carry the message intact under pressure.
20. CMI CORE DECLARATION
Yeshua is Salvation. His life is our life, His finished work is our standing, His Spirit is our indwelling witness and power, and His present Lordship is the government under which we live. We are believers — citizens formed in Christ, taught by Scripture, joined in commonwealth and sent into the nations. We live Eternal Life in Abundance: Zoe received in the Son, Perisson expressed through a life yielded to Him. We minister to the Lord, we test what we hear, we obey the summons we can establish, we carry one river through four streams, and we order our time so that rest, word, communion, service and mission remain visible. We are marked before we are armed, formed before we speak, and restrained before we wield an edge. Christ is the centre. Scripture is the witness. The Spirit glorifies the Son. The Son honours the Father. The Country belongs to the Father.

The public-facing identity of Believers Country is given in section 2.3. CMI public navigation and ministry naming use: Believers Country, School of Scriptures, Commonwealth, and Country Vanguard. This change is a public-label refinement, not a change to the one-river/four-streams framework.
